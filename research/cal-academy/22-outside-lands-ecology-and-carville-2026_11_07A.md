Date 2026-11-07. Web research only. Marks: [F] = page fetched and read this session; [S] = seen only as a search-result snippet; [unverified] = not fetched, or my own background knowledge, check before it goes on a label. ## 1. THE OUTSIDE LANDS BEFORE 1769 / 1848

### Extent and name
- "Outside Lands" = the 19th-century name for today's Richmond and Sunset districts; the 1866 Outside Lands Act (Congress, March 8, 1866) settled title and extended the city to the Pacific. Park land approved 1870; Hall appointed park engineer 1871. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outside_Lands
- An 1853 map labelled the west side the "Great Sand Bank" [F KQED]; the Chronicle later called it the "Great Sand Waste" [S]. https://www.kqed.org/news/11915008/golden-gate-park-was-once-miles-and-miles-of-sand-dunes
- Size: SFEI-linked pages say "around 1900" dunes covered roughly 22 square miles across the Sunset, Western Addition, Richmond, the park and much of the Presidio [S, Hidden Nature search summary]; another summary says "at least a dozen square miles" (SFEI's Sean Baumgarten) [S, secretsanfrancisco / Exploratorium result]. The two figures do not agree; use "12 to 22 square miles, sources differ". Heights: "some reached 80 feet or higher" [S]. No dune height figure found in a fetched page. [unverified]
- Forms (transverse ridges, swales, foredune) are standard dune geomorphology; I did not find a fetched source describing the SF dune forms in those words. The SFEI deck shows "Dune scrub", "Dwarf oak woodland" and "Interdune ponds and lakes" as the habitat classes. [F] https://www.spur.org/sites/default/files/2020-04/spur_presentation_try_this.pdf (Hidden Nature SF deck, read via local PDF text extraction)
- Mixed vegetated and bare dunes: "stretch upon stretch of soft dunes billowing clear to the horizon" (San Francisco Call, 4 April 1896, quoted in the SFEI deck) [F]. A Gold Rush letter of July 1850 calls the bay country "the most sterile and barren waste", with "continual smother of sand" (same deck) [F]. Another in the deck: sand hills near Kearny/Dupont, 1849, where horseshoes and metal were burnished by blown sand (Chever) [F].

### Plant communities (documented)
- SFEI Hidden Nature habitat classes for SF: dune scrub, dwarf oak woodland, interdune ponds and lakes (plus coastal prairie etc. elsewhere). 1856 quote: "Only clusters of chaparral and liveoak grew upon the sand dunes ... ponds and marshy places" around Eddy and Franklin (SF Call 1895, via deck). [F]
- Lake Merced: early accounts of wildflowers "so dense it was hard to avoid stepping on them"; sandy banks still hold dune plants. [S, search summary of funcheap/CNPS-type pages]
- Presidio Lobos Creek Valley restoration from 1994: dune rebuilt with native sand, 65,000 plants; rare annuals San Francisco lessingia, dune gilia, SF spineflower respond. [S] https://ecologycenter.org/lines-in-the-sand/ and https://presidio.gov/places/lobos-valley-overlook
- Sunset Scrub project (Presidio Trust): coastal buckwheat, dune tansy, lizard tail, about 2,000 plants. [S]
- NPS description: ground-hugging mats such as beach burr, yellow sand verbena, dune (beach) strawberry. [S; NPS page itself returned empty] https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090508021932/http://www.nps.gov/goga/naturescience/sanddunes.htm
- Yellow bush lupine: Hall tried it for the park; accounts disagree on whether it restrained sand. Barley then sea bent grass mixed with yellow lupin in the Panhandle trials. Caution: the 1870s planting used non-native grasses, and yellow bush lupine is itself invasive in some dune projects. [S] https://www.kqed.org/news/11915008 and https://goldengatepark.substack.com/p/the-father-of-golden-gate-park-its

### Lakes and creeks
- Lake Merced: named "Laguna de Nuestra Senora de la Merced" by Heceta 1775; Font's diary 1776 records many bears near the lake and a live-oak grove stood near it; Rancho Laguna de la Merced bought by Francisco de Haro 1837; Spring Valley Water Co. 1868; about 650 acres today; lost about 30 ft of water through a fissure in 1852; an 1881 map still shows an outlet to the sea. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Merced
- Lobos Creek: Presidio stream, mouth at Baker Beach (37.79093, -122.48534); fed by runoff and by the springs that form Mountain Lake; still the Presidio's drinking water (about 1 million gal/day diverted). [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobos_Creek
- "Dune Pond" photo c.1910, OpenSFHistory wnp15.346, shown on the Hidden Nature page. [F] https://sites.google.com/sfei.org/hidden-nature-sf/learn
- Mountain Lake, Laguna Honda, Pine Lake / Laguna Puerca, the Sunset's ponds: NOT found in a fetched source with detail. Wikipedia's Laguna Honda and Mountain Lake pages were not fetched. [unverified] Ask the GIS agent: the 1859 Coast Survey sheet and SFEI's georeferenced ~80 maps (majority 1850-1910) should show them. SFEI's deck says it georeferenced ~80 maps and ~210 photos. [F]

### Fauna
- SFEI Hidden Nature wildlife slide (extirpated mammals, year last documented): gray wolf 1827, bobcat 1827, tule elk 1827; American badger 1948; long-tailed weasel 1958; pronghorn, grizzly bear, black bear and Steller's sea cow listed as presumed or documented historical occurrence. California quail last documented 2018 (Golden Gate Park). [F deck; wildlife page [F] https://sites.google.com/sfei.org/hidden-nature-sf/learn/wildlife confirms tule elk extirpated from the peninsula, native grasses and soft scrub habitat]. So: tule elk are documented for SF, last record 1827; pronghorn is "presumed", not documented.
- Font 1776 diary frequently records tule elk and pronghorn on the Anza route and bears near the Laguna; Anza-Font full text: https://webdeanza.org/font76.html (not read; the specific SF entries are [unverified]). Crespi 1769 and Vancouver 1792 were not fetched. [unverified]
- Coyote, rabbits, wildcats and "innumerable quail" in the shrub cover of 1846 (Sacramento Daily Union 1871, in the deck). [F]
- Crissy Field lagoon shell midden: 84 species including sea otter, tule elk, grizzly bear. [S, search summary of Yelamu story map] https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/56b1d134920d46c6ac2462c1344eeb3f
- Academy early collections and Eastwood's SF flora notes: not found. [unverified]

### The Ramaytush Ohlone / Yelamu
- Villages named in the literature (Wikipedia, Milliken): Chutchui (summer, Mission Creek area), Sitlintac (Mission Bay edge), Petlenuc (near Crissy Field, Presidio), Amuctac (Visitacion Valley), Tubsinte (Candlestick), Ompuromo (southwest of Lake Merced), Timigtac and Pruristac (Pacifica area). [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yelamu
- Milliken's estimate: 160 to 300 Yelamu when the mission was founded on 30 June 1776; arrived 4,000 to 6,000 years ago per anthropologists; Mission baptisms 1777 to 1779. [F same page]. Disputed as an undercount by some. [S]
- Shell mounds near Lake Merced are noted. [S] No source found that describes dune foraging specifically. Do not invent routes through the dunes. [unverified]

### What happened to it
- 1871 grading, fencing, drainage, nursery; 1872 22,000 hardy fast trees planted. [F Wikipedia Outside Lands]
- Hall stabilised the lee side first, valley bottoms, with barley, lupine, manure; west end needed a sand-catching fence; by 1890 the park "looked fairly mature" (Christopher Pollock); McLaren from 1890 for about 50 years. [F KQED]
- The grid: 1900 Carville cars on dunes; 1906 earthquake pushes housing west; sidewalks and sewers replace dunes; Judah line to the beach 1928; Great Highway 1929 (seawall 1928, funded by the 1927 Boulevard Bond). [F Wikipedia Great Highway; F sanfranciscostory]
- Books to cite (not read): Raymond Clary, The Making of Golden Gate Park: The Early Years; Terence Young, Building San Francisco's Parks 1850-1930 (pp. 84-87 barley episode). [S]

Not found, not claimed: Joel Pomerantz Seep City, Pete Holloran, Peter Brastow writing, CNPS Yerba Buena chapter plant list, Alice Eastwood SF notes, Presidio Trust dune plan PDF (one result said parts were missing).

## 2. SPECIES LIST FOR THE DRAPE AND THE DOTS
Plants are chosen from the sourced communities above; the one-line looks are my botanical knowledge, check them against the Calflora/CNPS pages before labelling. [unverified for appearance]

Dune mat (foredune, ground-hugging)
1. Yellow sand verbena, Abronia latifolia: succulent mat, yellow ball flowers, spring to fall. Source: NPS mat list [S].
2. Beach burr, Ambrosia chamissonis: grey-green prostrate mat, burrs. Source: NPS [S].
3. Beach (dune) strawberry, Fragaria chiloensis: glossy dark leaves, white flowers, low, spring. Source: NPS [S].
4. Dune gilia, Gilia capitata ssp. chamissonis: low blue pom-pom heads, spring. Source: Presidio rare annuals [S].
5. San Francisco spineflower, Chorizanthe cuspidata var. cuspidata: tiny pink-white, prostrate, spring. Source: Presidio [S].

Dune scrub (semi-stable dunes)
6. Coast buckwheat, Eriogonum latifolium: grey rosettes, pink-white heads, summer. Source: Presidio Sunset Scrub [S].
7. Dune tansy, Tanacetum bipinnatum: grey ferny, yellow buttons, summer. Source: Presidio Sunset Scrub [S].
8. Beach sagewort, Artemisia pycnocephala: silver mounds to 60 cm. Photo on the sheet (CC BY-SA 3.0). Source: not in a fetched list; standard dune scrub plant. [unverified for SF dunes]
9. Yellow bush lupine, Lupinus arboreus: shrub 1 to 2 m, yellow spring spikes. Source: Hall's plantings [S]; native to the coast but invasive in some dune projects, so treat as the park's planting as much as the pre-colonial dunes. Photo on the sheet.
10. Coyote brush, Baccharis pilularis (dwarf form on dunes): dark green mounds, autumn white fluff. [unverified source]
11. San Francisco lessingia, Lessingia germanorum: low yellow-green, late summer yellow heads; endangered, Presidio. Source [S].

Coastal prairie and wet swales
12. Native bunchgrasses (needlegrass and fescues, Stipa/Festuca) on the Font-era hills: Font page for the East Bay says native bunchgrass hills. [S, ebparks] Applying it to the Outside Lands interdune is inference. [unverified]
13. Dune willow, Salix (arroyo/red willow) and tule, Schoenoplectus: swale and pond edge. SFEI shows "interdune ponds and lakes"; the willow/tule species are my inference. [unverified]
14. Live oak, Quercus agrifolia, wind-pruned: "dwarf oak woodland", Black Point, and a grove near Lake Merced in 1776. [F deck; F Wikipedia Lake Merced]

Animals
1. Tule elk, Cervus canadensis nannodes: tawny, dark neck, antlers; last SF record 1827. Source SFEI [F]. Photo on sheet (CC BY 2.0, Point Reyes area).
2. Grizzly bear, Ursus arctos californicus: Font 1776 bears near Lake Merced [F Wikipedia]; SFEI "presumed or documented" [F].
3. Pronghorn, Antilocapra americana: SFEI "presumed historical occurrence"; Font records them elsewhere [S]. Do not put on the dots as documented for SF.
4. Gray wolf, Canis lupus: last SF record 1827 [F SFEI]. 
5. Bobcat, Lynx rufus: last 1827 [F SFEI].
6. Coyote and brush rabbit: 1846 quote "cover of coyote and rabbits, wildcats" [F deck].
7. California quail, Callipepla californica: "innumerable quail" 1846 [F deck]; last SF record 2018 [F].
8. Silver digger bee, Habropoda miserabilis: dune specialist, rare in SF now [F wildlife page].
9. Sea otter and Steller sea lion: shell midden species and Lobos ("sea wolf") name; SFEI lists both [F].
10. Shore and lagoon birds (waterfowl, shorebirds): the Ohlone used waterfowl [S]; no species list found. [unverified]

## 3. CARVILLE-BY-THE-SEA

Sources: Wikipedia [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carville,_San_Francisco ; Woody LaBounty, San Francisco Story, 22 June 2022 [F] https://www.sanfranciscostory.com/carville-by-the-sea-in-10-slides ; search snippets for Atlas Obscura, Chronicle, Gizmodo, 7x7 (not fetched). Book: Woody LaBounty, Carville-by-the-Sea: San Francisco's Streetcar Suburb (2009), not read. Dates conflict between sources, noted below.

History
- 1893: Col. Charles Dailey and wife move to a hut on land rented from Adolph Sutro near Ocean Beach; he runs "The Annex" coffee saloon in a rented horsecar. [F Wikipedia]
- 12 Feb 1895: Market Street Railway ad sells car bodies, $20 with seats, $10 without. (Wikipedia says the ad ran in the SF Examiner.) [F both]
- Sutro bought several and rented on one of his blocks at about $5 a month; had provided no water, gas, sewer or sidewalks. [F sanfranciscostory]
- Falcons Bicycle Club: seven women cyclists and their admirers took a car, decorated for naps and dinners after rides in the park. Photo from Paul Melzer collection. [F sanfranciscostory]
- Musicians from downtown theatres: car "La Boheme", hill behind it nicknamed "Mount Diablo". [F]
- Jacob Heyman, real estate agent: bought a couple of dozen horse cars, one with each lot, built demonstration rentals, tapped groundwater. [F] Wikipedia: lots advertised 1896-97; a lot with two cars rented for $600 (1900). [F]
- Name "Carville-by-the-Sea" in use by 1897. [F Wikipedia]
- Size: LaBounty says about 200 cars by 1900; Wikipedia says estimated population 2,000 in 1900; another source says 50 families in 1901, 2,000 by 1908 [S]. Conflicting; cite as "about 200 cars (LaBounty)".
- Visitors/residents: Jack London, George Sterling, Gelett Burgess, Maisie Griswold, Xavier Martinez (studio in a car; painted "My Muse"). [F]
- 1906 fire and quake: displaced people move west; conventional houses; sidewalks and sewers; artists leave for Carmel. [F sanfranciscostory]
- 1913: the Oceanside Improvement Club holds a ceremonial burning of four cars; motto "Make clean today by sweeping and burning up the debris of yesterday." [F Wikipedia]. The brief says "burning 1913": confirmed. 
- 1920s: most gone, cars demolished, moved to backyards as sheds. A 1912 OpenSFHistory aerial looks southeast at Carville "shortly after it was cleared", near Irving and 48th [S]. Judah line to the beach 1928; about 30 cars still standing into the 1930s [S, gizmodo/7x7 summary]. The user-brief "last cars in the 1930s" is supported only by that snippet.

Location (several differing descriptions; none is an exact survey)
- Wikipedia: near 47th Ave and Lawton St, coordinates 37.75668 N, 122.50676 W. [F]
- LaBounty: c.1900 view roughly 47th Ave and Kirkham; 1890s block at 48th Ave and Lincoln Way; later cars along the Great Highway between Irving and Judah. [F]
- Extent map: none found. [unverified] A reasonable drape zone is the strip from Lincoln Way to Judah (and a bit south to Lawton), 46th to the Great Highway. Take the real outline from the 1899 Sanborn / 1915 Bain photos when the GIS agent has them. Note Wikipedia's coordinates plot further south than the Lincoln/48th description; the cluster moved over time.

What it looked like (images)
- Bain News Service photos, 1915, Library of Congress LCCN2014710022, -23, -24 (interior), -25: titled "Cartown (Calif.)"; on Commons, PD. Commons and Wikipedia associate them with Carville; the Bain captions themselves say Cartown. Contact sheet tiles 1 to 4. Good drape material (cars on bare sand with dune plants in the foreground).
- Commons also holds three near-duplicates labelled "Carville, San Francisco in 1920" (same Bain frames, so the 1920 label is doubtful; left off the sheet). A Commons file "Carville A + B Stations.jpg" is NOT Carville SF (shows smelter stacks); ignore.
- OpenSFHistory: photos wnp72.6181 (rear view, 1632 Great Highway, 1979, Greg Gaar), wnp26.1094 (horse car on S. Van Ness) etc. Terms not checked; left off the sheet. https://test.opensfhistory.org/photo/wnp72.6181 [S]. Request courtesy line: "Courtesy of the Western Neighborhoods Project / OpenSFHistory.org".
- Worden "Car Town on the Beach, S.F., 1901" (Bancroft Library), SFPL History Center, Randolph Brandt Collection, Jesse B. Cook Collection: cited in LaBounty's article; rights not verified, so not used. DigitalSF record "Carville" (digitalsf.org/record/27672) rights unstated. [S]

Survivors
- Wikipedia: only one structure widely known to survive, photo Feb 2018 (CC BY-SA 3.0, tile 5). [F]
- Atlas Obscura / search summaries: 1632 Great Highway, built 1908 from two cable cars (1880s) plus a horsecar side room (1870s); private residence; LaBounty's "Carville's Last Remnant" (Outside Lands, Dec 2002). [S, Atlas Obscura returned 403]. The Wikipedia photo shows a house with a garage fronting a street, not clearly the Great Highway; the match of address to the Commons photo is [unverified].
- The brief asked about "Carville houses on 47th Avenue": no source found. [unverified]

The beach of that time
- Park & Ocean Railroad: grading 5 Oct 1883, service 1 Dec 1883; Haight and Stanyan, diagonal along the park's south edge, Lincoln Way, then north along La Playa into the park's west end; steam (four 0-4-2T, four 2-4-2T Baldwin tank engines, "steam dummies"); merged into Market Street Railway Oct 1894, electrified 1899, later the 7 Haight. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_and_Ocean_Railroad . Note: it ran along Lincoln Way, i.e. right beside Carville's north edge.
- Sutro Baths: opened 14 March 1896; 499.5 x 254.1 ft; six salt pools and one fresh; burned June 1966 (arson). [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutro_Baths
- Beach Chalet: opened 1925, Willis Polk, replaced an 1892 chalet; Labaudt murals 1936-37. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_Chalet
- Great Highway: built 1929, seawall 1928, about 3.5 miles; the upper highway closed to private vehicles 14 March 2025 and became Sunset Dunes Park, opened 12 April 2025. [F] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Highway (Note the Wikipedia text also says an 1890s railway followed the route for the 1894 Midwinter Exposition, tracks removed 1895.)
- Cliff House (c.1900 and 1899 PD images on the sheet).

## 4. IDEAS FOR THE PLANET
- Three versions: pre-colonial (dunes, scrub, ponds, elk dots), 1900 (the park half-planted, Carville a scatter of box shapes at the sea edge, the Park & Ocean line a dot-train along Lincoln Way, Cliff House and Sutro Baths at the north tip), today.
- Moving dots for 1900: steam dummies on the P&O line, Dec 1883 to 1899; fewer cars from 1913.
- Drapes: Bain 1915 frames for the Carville patch; the 1865 "Sand Dunes of Golden Gate Park" (Young's San Francisco: a History) for the unplanted dune texture; the 1899 Detroit Photographic Cliff House color print for beach colour.
- Labels (one fact each, all [F] unless noted): "Great Sand Bank, 1853"; "Carville, 1895 to 1913, about 200 cars (LaBounty)"; "Sutro Baths 1896 to 1966"; "Park and Ocean Railroad 1883 to 1899"; "Last tule elk record in SF: 1827 (SFEI)"; "Beach Chalet 1925"; "Great Highway 1929"; "Lake Merced: Heceta 1775, bears seen by Font 1776".
- The elk are supportable (SFEI, 1827 last record); pronghorn and grizzly should be marked "presumed" if shown.
